*A counter-argument to myself* 

We are mostly surrounded by connectionist and statistical forms of artificial intelligence these days. Another theory Ibelieve in i.e. that these connectionist machines are implicitly implementing symbolc ai or turing-machines implictly also relies on a very 'behaviouralism' or 'functionalism' kind of intelligence. 

I have started to believe that human intelligence too is a collection of Turing Machines, and that too (I think) is a very functionalism idea. But if true intelligence requires semantic qualia-like understanding and manipulation of subject matter at hand? 

And these machines, if they are lacking in qualia, and if they're actually lacking 'semantic' understanding: would this rule them out from being truly intelligent? And then rule humans-in?


Informally, I feel like, if a machine is a collection of fixed TMs or algorithms, I find it quite difficult to believe that it will actually have any qualitative experience or qualia. Because most of these machines are doing is symbol pushing. This, of course brings us to to the hard problem: where and how does qualia come forth?

With neural nets, perhaps because of their internals being somewhat mysterious, I feel like maybe there is a chance that they could possibly implicitly have qualia?

Could intelligence be a 'meaning'-oriented phenomenon? Tied up with the a animal's survival and their capacity for meaning? 

When I am going through a hard chapter in a next book or a hard logical problem, and unable to parse it's 'meaning' quickly --> maybe that is what is intelligence (or it's lack). The subjective feeling of 'understanding' something. 

Could that be explained away by the TM-idea? If I am getting stuck in a logical problem, or a programming puzzle, maybe I do not have the right 'mental' algorithm to parse the problem correctly? maybe the problem or the textbook hasn't described it in a eloquent manner? maybe I am tired? 

But then, is 'understanding' then a huge component of intelligence? I am re-thinking everything.
