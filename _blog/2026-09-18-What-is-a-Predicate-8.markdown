"A predicate is a statement with blanks that become true or false when we 
fill them in." More formally, it represents a statement that represents a 
relation. P(a) is an atomic formula, where P is the predicate, and a is 
"filled" in P the predicate, hence creating a logical formula.

These are a fundamental notion in first-(and higher)-order-logic, because 
they let us open up the atoms, peer inside them. This isn't the case with 
propositional logic, where there are no free variables; and where atoms are 
indivisible.
